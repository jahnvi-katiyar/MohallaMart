# Problem Analysis

## Problem Statement

**Shop Product Catalog & Inquiry Hub**

Neighborhood shops often depend on physical footfall, phone calls, WhatsApp messages, and word-of-mouth to showcase their products. Customers have limited visibility into what local shops actually have in stock before visiting them.

The core gap is:

> **Local shops have products, but customers cannot easily discover and inquire about those products digitally.**

---

## Top 5 Real-World Friction Points

### 1. Customers Don't Know What Is Available

**Friction:**  
Customers cannot easily check whether a nearby shop has a particular product before visiting.

**Root Cause:**
- No digital product catalog
- Stock information is not publicly visible
- Local shops primarily depend on physical discovery

**Current Workaround:**
- Call the shop
- Send a WhatsApp message
- Ask friends/family
- Visit the shop physically

**Impact:**
- Wasted customer time
- Unnecessary travel
- Missed sales opportunities

---

### 2. Product Information Is Scattered

**Friction:**  
Product photos, prices, sizes, colors, and availability are often shared individually through conversations rather than maintained in one structured place.

**Root Cause:**
- No simple digital storefront
- Small shops may not have dedicated websites
- Existing e-commerce platforms can be unnecessarily complex

**Current Workaround:**
- Shopkeeper sends product photos through WhatsApp
- Customer asks for price separately
- Customer asks again about size/color/availability

**Impact:**
- Repetitive communication
- Difficult product discovery
- Inconsistent information

---

### 3. Customer Inquiries Are Difficult to Track

**Friction:**  
Shopkeepers receive inquiries through multiple channels such as calls, WhatsApp, social media, and in-person conversations.

**Root Cause:**
- No centralized inquiry system
- Conversations are disconnected
- No structured inquiry status

**Current Workaround:**
- Search through WhatsApp chats
- Check call history
- Remember pending customers manually

**Impact:**
- Missed inquiries
- Delayed responses
- Potential loss of customers

---

### 4. Updating Product Availability Is Time-Consuming

**Friction:**  
Even when a shopkeeper creates an online listing, keeping prices and stock information current can become additional work.

**Root Cause:**
- Manual catalog maintenance
- Products frequently sell out or change price
- Small shopkeepers have limited time for digital administration

**Current Workaround:**
- Update customers individually
- Tell customers the latest availability through phone/WhatsApp
- Avoid maintaining a catalog altogether

**Impact:**
- Outdated product information
- Customer confusion
- Reduced trust in online listings

---

### 5. Customers Must Contact Multiple Shops to Find One Product

**Friction:**  
When a product is unavailable at one shop, customers often have to repeat the same search with several other local shops.

**Root Cause:**
- No centralized local product discovery
- Shops operate independently
- Inventory is not easily searchable across stores

**Current Workaround:**

```text
Shop A → Call → Not Available
Shop B → WhatsApp → Not Available
Shop C → Visit → Available
```

**Impact:**
- Repeated effort
- Extra travel
- Delayed purchase
- Frustration for customers

---

## Core Opportunity

The problem is not simply that local shops lack e-commerce.

The deeper problem is the lack of a **simple digital bridge between local inventory and local demand**.

A solution should therefore focus on:

**Product Discovery → Availability → Inquiry → Communication → Store Visit**

rather than attempting to immediately replace traditional offline commerce.
